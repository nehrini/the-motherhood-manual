The last few posts have been heavy. Honest, but heavy. So this one is lighter, because the first months of parenthood are also, frankly, ridiculous. Here are my greatest innovations, and how each of them was crushed.

## The breakthrough

It started with a simple problem. Our home looked like a tornado had hit it, and I had a baby who only wanted to be held and LOVED white noise.

Then it came to me. What if I wore the baby... and vacuumed at the same time?

Two birds, one Dyson. The house got cleaner. The baby fell asleep. I was, quite frankly, a genius. I couldn't wait to tell someone.

So I told my dad.

He nodded and explained, very calmly, that this is what they did before white noise machines were invented.

And there it was. My one moment of maternal brilliance, and the previous generation had figured it out decades ago, with no app, no gadget and no Bluetooth speaker.

## Prior art

It turns out my dad and my in-law's have quite the back catalogue of discoveries we thought were ours.

**Babies like to sleep in cars.** They knew. Of course they knew.

**Babies don't need total silence during the day.** Also known. Apparently there was never any need to tiptoe around the house like we were defusing a bomb.

At this point, I've stopped filing patents. I just ring my dad first.

## Back in my day

My favourite piece of generational wisdom, delivered over a video call:

*"Back in my day, we just let babies kick about. None of this fancy gadgetry."*

He's not wrong. He's not exactly right either. But he's not wrong.

## Skills I now have

Parenthood has given me abilities I never knew I could develop.

**I am now ambidextrous.** I type with my left hand. I eat standing up, one-handed, over the sink, like a raccoon. Babywearing is simply how we live now.

**I'm getting my weighted squats in.** Every bounce, every pick-up, every "please just go to sleep" lap of the kitchen. Who needs a gym?

**Speed nappy changes.** Having a boy, I quickly learned, means there's roughly a 50% chance you'll get wee'd on during any given nappy change. I learned this the way most parents do: through direct experience. I can now change a nappy faster than a Formula One pit crew, and I'd like it on my CV.

## Overengineering parenthood

When you put two people who work in tech in charge of a newborn, things get... thorough.

My husband built a weekly tracker that tells us what to expect each week, how much the baby should be eating, and so on. Our ancestors raised children for thousands of years without a single dashboard. We lasted about four days.

## Autopilot

Recently, I was out walking the dog. No baby. Just me, the dog, and a passer-by chatting a little loudly on the footpath.

I shushed them.

Not loudly enough for them to hear, thankfully. But loudly enough that I had to stop and laugh at myself in the middle of the street.

## Calculus at 2am

My husband has many talents. One of them is now writing original songs for an audience of one. The lyrics are... improvised. The tunes are... evolving. The baby seems to like them, so who are we to judge?

But my favourite memory is watching him take on the swaddle at 2am for the first time. The concentration on his face. The folding, and unfolding, and refolding. He looked like a man who had just been handed a calculus problem in a language he didn't speak.

He got there in the end. The baby was swaddled. Mostly.

## Looking for the adult in the room

The running joke in our house is this: whenever something happens, we both instinctively look around for the adult in the room who's going to sort it out.

Then we remember. It's us. We're the adults in the room.
{: .pullquote}

Nobody warned us about that part, either.
