There are two of us, one dog, and now one very small baby. Which makes this our first Christmas as a family of four. Full hearts full bellies and Mariah Carey.

## Christmas, before

Before he arrived, Christmas usually meant one of two things. Either we travelled to see my in-laws, where I would happily eat my body weight in cakes and sweets, or our families came to us here in Ireland.

Either way, my December followed the same ritual: Christmas movies, carols and Christmas songs on repeat. My family is, at this point, thoroughly sick of Mariah Carey every December. I regret nothing.

## The great tree debate

Every year in our house there is a battle over when the lights and the tree go up.

My husband says the 1st of December.

I say the end of October is already too late.

We have not yet reached a compromise. I suspect this year, with a baby to show it all to, I might finally win.

## Traditions worth passing on

There are some recipes my husband grew up with that my mother-in-law has taught me, and I can't wait to make them for our son one day.

I want to watch all the Christmas movies with him. Fill his stocking. Take him to see the Christmas lights. They still give me that sense of wonder, even now, and I want him to feel it too.

## This year

This year, it'll be just us. A family of four under our tree, with carols playing and something baking in the oven.

We've already bought him his very own Christmas jumper, so there will be holiday photos. Many holiday photos.

He has his first Christmas outfit, his first ornament and his first stocking. My husband has a 3D printer, so our son is also getting more custom-made Christmas things than any baby could possibly need. I've stopped asking what's being printed in the spare room.

## Love across the miles

With no family in Ireland, Christmas Day for us always includes video calls. We ring everyone, wish them a happy Christmas, open presents on camera, and turn the phone around so they can see the tree.

This year they'll be seeing him too.

It isn't the same as being in the same room. But the love is still felt, every bit of it, right through the screen.

## The fourth member of the family

Our dog takes Christmas very seriously. She has a festive bow for her collar and a Christmas-themed harness, and every year she gets her own presents under the tree: stuffies, chew toys and treats.

She's remarkably calm around the tree. Her only vice is that she likes to gently boop the baubles with her nose, and then sit back and watch them swing.

But the thing that melts me most is how she's taken to our son. She's been thrilled with him from the start. She stays with us through everything: tummy time, the witching hour, every feed. And when he cries, she brings over her favourite toy and tries to give it to him.

Her love is boundless, and I'm in awe of it every single day.

## Christmas carols, remixed

My husband hasn't composed any Christmas songs yet. I, however, have started singing our son's babbles back to him to the tunes of Christmas carols.

The choirs of Ireland would be horrified. He seems to like it.

## A heart full enough to burst

I wasn't always sure I would have children. Not until I met my husband, anyway.

So this first Christmas, in our little family of four, has my heart so full it could burst.
{: .pullquote}

## A new tradition

This year we're starting something new: a family photo by the tree, every Christmas, kept safe so that when I'm old and grey I can look back at all of them.

Because I know it already. These are the good old days I'm living in.
