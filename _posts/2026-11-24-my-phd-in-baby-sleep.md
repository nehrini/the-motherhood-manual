I'm writing this at 4am, holding my son, trying very hard not to fall asleep mid-sentence. He, on the other hand, is wide awake and absolutely delighted about it.

Welcome to the sleep regression. Or what I think is the sleep regression. Honestly, I'm not even sure.

## The night it all fell apart

One minute, he was sleeping beautifully. We had a routine. We had a book of tricks. We were, dare I say it, a little bit smug.

The next, he was waking every 30 to 45 minutes, crying, and every single trick we had stopped working overnight.

So I did what I always do at 3am. I googled. And the internet handed me two words: *sleep regression*.

## What it looks like in our house

- Waking every hour, on the hour, like a very small, very loud alarm clock.
- Naps? Only on me. Contact naps or nothing.
- And my personal favourite: waking up at 3am, completely delighted with himself, babbling away like it's the middle of the afternoon.

## The research phase

As two engineers, we did what we know best. We researched.

Articles at 2am. Sleep charts. Wake windows. Reddit threads. AI. My dad. All of them.

I now know more about infant sleep than I ever knew about anything I studied for either of my master's degrees. I'm fairly confident I've earned a PhD. The thesis is still pending, mostly because the research subject refuses to co-operate.

## The thermostat

Of all our experiments, the room temperature has received the most attention.

Our thermostat is now monitored more closely than a NOC dashboard on breaking-change release day. (If you work in tech, you'll get this one. If you don't, just imagine a lot of people staring very anxiously at a screen, waiting for something to go wrong.)

## Peer review

You might expect two Type A engineers to have competing theories, heated debates and a whiteboard covered in sleep data.

No. We're both just confused and very tired. We've moved on to a new methodology: taking it as it comes.

## Results

After extensive research, I'm pleased to present our findings.

The baby ignored the literature.
{: .pullquote}

He has done exactly as he pleases, and right now, at 4am, as I bounce him gently and hope, he seems mighty thrilled with himself.

## The night shift

We work in shifts. One of us takes the couch so the other can get a real three-hour window of sleep. It's a good system, on paper.

In practice, as the primary food source, I seem to have drawn the short end of that particular stick.

## Field notes from the sleep-deprived

The other morning, I was making coffee to keep myself awake. Halfway through, I decided I could also turn on the dishwasher.

I now have a dishwasher load of clean dishes, and one very clean coffee pod.

No coffee, though.

## The worry, and the giggles

Some nights I worry that something's bothering him. That I'm missing a sign, or doing something wrong.

Other nights, at 2am, while we're trying everything we can think of to get him back to sleep, he's lying there babbling away, perfectly content, and I can't help but laugh.

## What we've actually learned

When it passes, I promise I'll come back and share all our secrets. (I'm not holding my breath on that, but I promise.)

Until then, what we've learned is this. He's working through so much growth and development right now. So, as we have since the very first day, we just hold him and love him until he decides it's time to sleep.

People complain about the lack of sleep that comes with being a parent. And yes, I'm exhausted. But honestly? You'd do it all again.
