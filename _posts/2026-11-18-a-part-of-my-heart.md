I've written a lot here about how hard the beginning was. The confusion, the contradicting advice, the 3am panic. All of that was real, and I don't want to pretend otherwise.

But it isn't the whole story. It isn't even most of it. This post is about the rest.

## The moment I met him

I remember lying on the operating table while they stitched me up, with this tiny human on my chest. All I could feel was awe.

How was it possible that I had made this person?

Ever since that moment, right alongside the fear of how to do right by him, there has been a fierce love that has only grown. I finally understand what people mean by "mama bear".

## Long nights, short years

There's a saying that parenthood is long nights and short years. It has never felt more true.

I can't believe how fast it's going. My tiny newborn, who couldn't open his eyes or focus on anything, now babbles at me, holds my gaze and smiles at me. Sometimes I feel a little sad at how quickly it's all slipping by. But on the hard days, it helps to remember that this is exactly what's supposed to happen. He's growing. He's thriving.

## All the little things

If you asked me what melts me about him, I'd say all of it.

The little babbles and coos. The funny faces he pulls in his sleep. The pouty little kissy face he makes when he stretches. The way he smells, which I wish I could bottle up and keep forever.

And his hand around my finger. I know it's a reflex. We researched every newborn reflex he was supposed to have, and tested them all. But every single time his tiny fingers curl around mine, it makes every minute of this feel worth it.

## Our mornings

My favourite time with him is the morning after he's had a good stretch of sleep.

He's alert and bright. He wants to coo and chat and look right at me. He smiles now, and he loves being cuddled. In those mornings, it really feels like he knows his mum is there.

## The first smile

His first smile brought me to tears.

It wasn't just that he smiled. It was that he looked at me, made eye contact, and smiled at *me*. I don't think I'll ever be able to properly describe the joy of that moment. I'm not sure there are words for it.

## Who I'm becoming

Becoming his mother has shown me how unconditional love can be.

It's made me stronger, and more protective. I'm more willing to stand up for him than I ever was for myself.

It's also shown me a softness I didn't know I had. A capacity to carry this enormous love for another person. To want to slow down for him, make time for him, and just hold him and love him.

## I can actually do this

There wasn't one big moment where I suddenly felt like I knew what I was doing. It was lots of small ones, adding up.

The first time I soothed him when nothing else would console him. The first night I managed to transfer him to his cot without waking him. The first time I understood, just from watching him, that he was done playing and ready to sleep.

Somewhere along the way, I learned to give myself the grace I'd give any other parent. That changed everything.

## What this love feels like

It's bigger than anything I've ever known. And different, too.

It feels like there's a part of my heart that will never fully belong to me again.
{: .pullquote}

It's tied to everything he does and everything he feels.

I want what's best for him. I want to keep him safe and warm and loved. And in return, I barely notice the tired arms, the aching back from bouncing him, or the sleepless nights.

Before I had him, I was someone who valued sleep and time to myself above almost anything. Of everything about this transition, that change has surprised me the most.

## Watching him become a dad

Watching my husband become a father has made me love him more.

The moment that sticks with me is seeing him cry as our son was born. Since then, I've watched a tenderness in him that I didn't know existed. It's a wonderful thing, watching the person you married become someone's dad.

## What it's done for us

Becoming parents has made us respect each other's qualities more: the love, the patience, the care.

It's shown each of us that the other notices the little things, and steps in without being asked. I didn't expect that a season this exhausting could make our marriage stronger. But it has.

## Home, far from home

We don't have family in Ireland, but we haven't been alone. Friends have checked in and offered support. Our public health nurse called in and told us we were doing a good job. It's hard to explain how much a sentence like that can mean when you're running on no sleep. Every bit of it helped.

Our families haven't met him in person yet, only on video calls. He's the first grandchild on both sides, and the love that has poured in from across the world has been incredible, along with the presents. We can already see how much his grandparents are going to spoil him, and I can't wait.

## What I'm looking forward to

Watching him grow, and become exactly the person he's meant to be. And just loving him, every step of the way.

## If you're in the hard part right now

If you've read the earlier posts in this series and recognised yourself in the confusion and the fear, I want you to know this part is coming for you too, if it isn't already there.

The hard days are real. So is this. And this is the part that stays.

## To my son

One day, when you're grown and you read this, I want you to know one thing:

*You were always the reason for my existence.*
