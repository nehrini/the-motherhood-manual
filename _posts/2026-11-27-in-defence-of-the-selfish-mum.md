I'm going to say something that might sound a little controversial for a new mum.

Be selfish.

Not all the time. Not at your baby's expense. But in those first months, when every minute of every day seems to belong to someone else, I think it's not only okay to claim a little time back for yourself. I think it's essential.

## You can't pour from an empty cup

We've all heard it. It's on mugs and posters and Instagram tiles. But I don't think I really understood it until I became a mum.

Our children need us to be full: full of happiness, full of love, full of energy, so that we can pour all of that into giving them a joyful childhood. And you simply can't do that when you're running on empty.

Looking after yourself isn't taking something away from your baby. It's how you make sure there's enough of you to go around.
{: .pullquote}

## What "selfish" looks like for me

It's nothing grand. It's mostly small things that make me feel like a person again.

**Moving my body.** Before my son, I was always outdoors, walking, hiking and dancing. After a C-section, I had to start again, gently and at my own pace. I've prioritised the workouts that help my core heal and make me feel like me again. Every small bit of strength I get back feels like a win.

**A proper shower.** Not a thirty-second rinse while listening for the baby. A real shower, with my favourite scents, where I can actually breathe for a few minutes.

**A great cup of coffee.** Even at home. Even if I have to reheat it twice. Making a really good cup of coffee, just the way I like it, is a tiny daily ritual that makes the whole morning feel better.

**Fresh air.** Getting out for a walk every day, with Goldie and the pram, does more for my head than almost anything else.

**Ten minutes that are just mine.** Writing this blog. Reading with a cup of tea while he naps on me. Doing my nails, even if they only last a day.

## The happiest mums I know

When I think about the happiest mums I've met, they have something in common.

They still have their own hobbies. Their own workouts. Their own time. They haven't disappeared into motherhood. They've brought all of themselves into it.

Their kids aren't missing out because of that. If anything, they're getting the best version of their mum.

## Letting go of the guilt

I know the guilt that comes with taking time for yourself. That little voice that says you should be doing something for the baby, or the house, or someone else.

But I've started to see it differently. When I take care of myself, I come back to my son more patient, more present and more joyful. Every time.

And I couldn't do any of it without my husband taking the baby so I can go, and doing the same for him in return. We both need our cups filled. Parenting is a team sport.

## If you're the partner reading this

You have more power here than you might think.

Most new mums won't ask for time to themselves. We feel guilty, or we think we should be able to do it all, or we're simply too tired to work out what we need. So don't wait to be asked.

Take the baby and tell her to go. Run the shower, put on the coffee, hand her the trainers or the book. Protect that time like it matters, because it does. And when she comes back, don't make her feel she owes you for it.

Then let her do the same for you. You need your cup filled too. A happy, rested partner on both sides is the best thing you can give your baby.

## They're watching

Here's the part that matters most to me.

Our children learn far more from what we do than from what we say. If I want my son to grow up knowing it's okay to look after himself, to have passions, to move his body, to make time for the things that light him up, then I have to show him.

So make your child your motivation. Live your best life, so they can watch you and do the same.
