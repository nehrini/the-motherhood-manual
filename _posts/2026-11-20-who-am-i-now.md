Before he arrived, a typical Saturday looked something like this.

A long walk in nature with my dog, then probably another one later. Coffee and a wander around the shops with friends. Something good in the oven, because I loved cooking and baking. My hair done, my nails done. A book. Dinner with my husband and a bottle of wine, sometimes a good movie and a long conversation. Other times just lying around watching our favourite shows and laughing. And, if the mood was right, dancing till 2am.

I was outdoorsy and active, and I always had time for the things that made me feel like me.

## The two-hour window

The thing I miss most isn't any one of those things. It's the feeling of having time.

Now every day feels like a series of two-hour windows, and in each one I have to make a choice. Do I sleep? Shower? Cook something? Walk the dog? I can usually pick one. On a good day, two.

The days blend together in a gentle blur of feeds, nappies and appointments. I haven't had a dramatic moment of not recognising my life. It's more that the edges of each day have gone soft.

## What I thought I'd lose

Before he was born, I was quietly afraid I'd lose my sense of identity. That I'd disappear into being "his mum" and forget who I was.

That isn't what happened.

Motherhood didn't take away who I am. It brought out more of me.
{: .pullquote}

## Learning to live in a different body

This part has been harder.

I was someone who moved all the time. Even through my pregnancy I was dancing and walking for around two hours a day. Going from that to finding bending down and climbing the stairs a challenge has been a real struggle, physically and mentally.

Some days my body feels and looks a little alien to me. The softness that's still there. The marks that weren't there before.

I once saw a show where a woman was sad about her stretch marks from pregnancy, and her little girl was fascinated by them. She thought they looked like tiger stripes. I think about that a lot.

Because this body grew my son. It birthed him. And now it sustains him. When I stop and really think about that, it's nothing short of a miracle. I'm learning to be grateful to it, rather than critical of it.

## On maternity leave

I'd be lying if I said I didn't feel guilty sometimes for not working.

But mostly I feel relief, real relief, that Ireland gives mothers six months of maternity leave. Six months to recover, to learn who my son is, and to simply be with him.

I do wonder about going back. I was always someone who was happy to put in the late nights and the weekend hours when they were needed. Work was always the first thing I was focused on. I don't know that's who I'll be anymore, and I'm still working out how I feel about that.

## The career I built

Right now, my career and hobbies feel a little distant from who I am day to day. Most days, I just feel like Mum.

But I've always loved what I do and the field I work in. I hope that when I go back, I'll feel more like myself again in some ways. And I truly believe that a happy, fulfilled mother means my son gets the best version of me.

## The new me

If you asked me what I like most about who I am now, I'd say it's discovering how much patience and joy I'm capable of. I didn't know I had this much of either.

## Ten minutes for me

I don't have Saturdays like I used to. But I've found small pockets.

About ten minutes a day, I write this blog. It makes me feel like I'm doing something that's mine.

And when he falls asleep on me, I try to read with a cup of tea beside me. Sometimes that's all it takes to reset the whole mood of the day.

## If you feel like you've lost yourself

If you're a few weeks in and you feel like you don't know where you are or what you're doing, I promise that's normal.

Trust the process. And make time for yourself, even if it's just five minutes a day.

Your baby only gets one mum. Make sure she's happy and healthy too.
