Is he smiling enough? Should he be holding his head up by now? Is he following things with his eyes? Babbling? Sleeping longer stretches?

If you're a new parent, I'd guess at least one of those questions has crossed your mind this week. For me, honestly, it's been all of them.

## When the wondering starts

I deleted social media early on, partly to protect myself from exactly this. And still, the questions find me.

Sometimes it's when he does something new, and I find myself wondering what's supposed to come next. Sometimes it's watching him struggle with a new skill, like lifting his head during tummy time, his whole little body working so hard. And sometimes it's when the GP runs through the milestone questions at a check-up, and my brain quietly starts keeping score.

## Two Type A parents

I'll be honest about our part in this.

Before he arrived, my husband and I read extensively about parenting. We researched his whole first year. When he was born, we looked up every newborn reflex he was supposed to have, and tested them all.

Was that our engineer brains, or our worried-parent brains? A bit of both, if I'm honest. It's what happens when two Type A personalities marry each other and then have a baby.

## Where the pressure comes from

Talking to other mums, I've realised most of the milestone pressure doesn't come from doctors or nurses. It comes from social media, and sometimes from other mums.

It's the offhand comments. "Mine was already crawling by then." "Oh, we started weaning early." "She was smiling at four weeks." They're usually said without a second thought. But for a first-time mum who's already unsure of everything, a comment like that can land like a weight.

Nobody has said anything like that to me yet. If they did, I'd shut it down very quickly. My baby will develop at his own pace, and I'll support him in whatever way he needs.

## Why I stopped comparing

I made a conscious choice early on. I removed social media, and I haven't been sharing much about what he's up to. Not because I'm not proud of him, I'm endlessly proud of him, but because I didn't want to start the spiral of comparison, for me or for anyone else.

Every baby develops at their own pace. They should be guided, nurtured and celebrated for who they are.

## What helps me

Paying less attention to opinions, and stepping away from social media, have helped more than I can say.

Mostly, I just focus on what he's doing today. Not what he "should" be doing, or what someone else's baby did at this age. Just today.

And on the days the worry creeps in anyway, I remind myself of one thing:

Whatever pace he chooses is just fine.
{: .pullquote}

## To the parent reading this

If you've found yourself counting weeks, comparing, or lying awake wondering whether your baby is "behind", I see you. I've been there too.

So here's my gentle reminder, from one new parent to another: slow down, and enjoy the baby in front of you. It's not a race, and your baby is unique.
