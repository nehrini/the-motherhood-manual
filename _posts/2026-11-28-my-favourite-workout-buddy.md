In my last post, I wrote about why it's okay, and even important, to make time for yourself as a new mum. Moving my body is a big part of that for me.

But here's what I've discovered: my workouts are so much more enjoyable when I bring my son along.

## The original workout buddy

It started with the simplest thing: holding him while I walk. Around the house, around the garden, out on our local loops. Every step with him in my arms or in the carrier counts.

From there, I got a little more creative.

**Before you try any of this: please make sure you've been cleared for exercise by your care providers, whether that's your GP, consultant, midwife or physio. Every recovery is different, especially after a C-section.**
{: .pullquote}

## The baby workout menu

**Babywearing Pilates.** With him snuggled into the carrier, I can move slowly and with control, and he usually drifts off to sleep.

**Kiss push-ups.** He lies on his play mat underneath me, and every time I lower down, he gets a kiss. He finds this hilarious. I find it motivating.

**Peek-a-boo planks.** Same setup, same reward system. It's amazing how much longer you can hold a plank when there's a babbling, smiling face below you.

**Glute bridges with a passenger.** I sit him up on me, supported, and he babbles away at me while I lift and lower. He thinks it's a ride. I think it's a workout. We're both right.

## Baby bachata

This one is my absolute favourite, and it's personal.

My husband and I met at a salsa class. We've been dancing, and teaching salsa and bachata in Dublin, for ages now. Dance is how our story started.

So now, with our son held close, I dance bachata around the kitchen. A gentle sway, a few basic steps, the music low. He loves the rhythm. More often than not, he's asleep before the song ends.

It's a workout, a lullaby and a little piece of his parents' love story, all at once.
{: .pullquote}

## Not exactly Instagram-worthy

Let me be clear: none of this looks like the polished workout videos on social media.

There's spit-up. There are pauses for nappy changes. There are planks that end early because someone needs a cuddle right now. Sometimes a full routine turns into ten minutes of swaying and a cuddle.

And that's completely fine. This isn't about looking a certain way. It's about recovering, getting stronger, and feeling like myself again.

Every step counts.

## What he gets out of it

I love that he gets to experience this version of his mum. A mum who's moving, laughing and dancing. A mum who gives him kisses and cuddles between reps, and sways him to sleep to music.

I hope that one day, moving his body will feel as natural and joyful to him as it does to me. I hope he'll remember it as something fun, not a chore. Something we did together.

## What I get out of it

Motivation, mostly. On the days I'm too tired to even think about exercise, he's my reason to start. Five minutes of kiss push-ups or one bachata song is still five minutes more than nothing.

And I get my workout in without losing a minute of time with him.

## If you're the partner reading this

You can join in too. Dance with the baby. Take them on a long walk. Swap in for the planks so she can do a full set with both hands free.

Or, my personal recommendation: dance with both of them. Some of my favourite moments since he was born have been the three of us swaying in the kitchen together.

## Make them your motivation

If you're a new mum trying to find your way back to moving your body, start small, go at your own pace, and bring your little one along.

It won't look perfect. It doesn't need to. Every step counts in our recovery.
