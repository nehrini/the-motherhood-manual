If you've been reading along, you'll have noticed someone pops up in almost every post. She sniffs nappies, boops baubles and sighs at us at 3am.

It's time she got her own chapter. Meet my Goldie.

## How she chose me

A friend of mine had adopted her, but couldn't keep her, and asked if we'd like to take her. We went to meet her. She walked straight over, sat down in my lap, and that was it. I was hers.

To say she's spoiled is an understatement. She has toys, she has homemade treats, and I own entire recipe books just for her. She's my hiking buddy, my 2am cuddle bear and my best friend.

She was my first baby, long before I had a second one.

## She knew before we did

Goldie noticed I was pregnant before we did.

I had always been her rough-and-tumble playmate. Almost overnight, she turned gentle with me. She became watchful. She followed me from room to room, checking I was okay. On walks, she didn't love other dogs coming up to me, or strangers getting too close.

She'd clocked it. She was already on duty.

## Basic training

We didn't leave her preparation to chance. We started training her to walk beside the pram when I was two months pregnant. Every afternoon, we played baby sounds while she got treats, so crying would mean good things were coming.

We've trained her since she was young, so she already had a rock-solid "place" and "leave it", and they've been an absolute blessing. We still do about fifteen minutes of training before each of her meals. It's also our dedicated time together.

Walk, play and train: in my experience, those are the foundations of a happy, content dog.

## The big day

When we came home from the Rotunda, I went in first, without the baby. I spent ten minutes just playing with her, because I had missed my fur baby so much during those days in hospital.

Then my husband carried in the car seat.

All that training paid off. Four paws on the floor. She sniffed his little feet, very politely. You could see the excitement in her full-body wiggles, but she stayed calm.

Then she followed him everywhere we carried him. She still does.

## The moment she accepted him

That first night, after coming over to give me some love, she curled up and fell asleep right beside his bassinet.

That's when I knew she'd accepted this little newcomer. More than accepted him.

She'd decided he was hers too.
{: .pullquote}

## Her new job description

Goldie has taken on a lot of new responsibilities.

- **Feed attendant:** present for every single feed.
- **Tummy time supervisor:** lies nearby, watching closely.
- **Witching hour support staff:** stays with us through the hardest hour of the day.
- **Head of pram security:** alert and watchful whenever anyone walks past the pram.
- **Chief entertainment officer:** her tail is officially his favourite mobile.
- **Crisis response:** when he cries, she brings him her favourite toy.
- **Health and safety:** when my husband lifts the baby up high or swings him down low, she barks at him to stop. It's a sentiment I fully echo.

## How he feels about her

There's no grabbing yet, thankfully. But he babbles at her and smiles at her, and when she sits near him, he turns to look for her. It's all very, very sweet.

## Did she lose her place?

Not for a second. We were adamant that nothing would change for her. She keeps all her privileges.

She does get a bit less of our attention sometimes. We can't always play the moment she wants to, and she's learning a lot of patience, especially while I take hours to leave the house. But she still gets her one-on-one time with each of us, every day.

There's no jealousy at all. Just the occasional very pointed sigh by the front door.

## The friendship I hope they'll have

I grew up with dogs, and the love of animals is something I'll carry until the day I die.

I hope he learns kindness and patience from her. I hope he learns that love doesn't always need words. I hope they play together and grow together.

Mostly, I hope he finds his best friend too.
