I've lived in Ireland for over a decade, so I thought I knew the weather here. Then I tried to take a newborn out in it.

## Leaving the house: then and now

**Before the baby:** keys, rain jacket, sunglasses. Out the door. The dog requires zero preparation. She loves the rain, and we just blow-dry her when we get home.

**Now:** the pram. The rain cover. The nappy bag, which must contain nappies, a full change of clothes, burp cloths, dummies and wipes. Then the baby, in whatever number of layers I've decided is correct today. Then the dog, who has been waiting by the door this entire time with an expression of deep betrayal.

She's still ready in four seconds. I am not.

## The layers question

I am always, always worried he's either overdressed or underdressed. There is no in-between.

On a typical day he's in a babygro, with an outdoor fleece suit on top. If he's asleep, it's a sleep sack and the pram insert. And then there are the hats, which seem to have been designed specifically to not stay on a baby's head.

At least once a walk, I lean into the pram to check he's not too hot, then check he's not too cold, then check again, just in case.

## Never trust the weather app

After more than ten years here, I've learned the most important lesson of Irish life: the weather app is lying to you.

So I no longer check it. I just go out prepared for everything. Hurricane? I have the rain cover. Drought? I have sunglasses. Both within twenty minutes? Of course. This is Ireland.

## Gone with the wind

It's nearly winter, and the real enemy isn't the rain. It's the wind.

More than once, I've felt the pram start to drift gently sideways, like it's decided to go on its own little adventure. Add a bouncy dog who is absolutely delighted by the rain, and you get a combination I can only describe as not naturally compatible.

## The dog has been trained (mostly)

To be fair to her, she's very good. We started training her to walk beside the pram when I was two months pregnant, long before there was anyone in it. We still make sure she gets one long walk a day with just one of us, so she can run off all that energy.

She's brilliant with the pram now. Except for the odd time she forgets it's there, glances over, and gets a genuine fright at this large wheeled object that has apparently appeared out of nowhere. Every single time.

If there's one takeaway from this post, it's this: if you have a dog and a baby on the way, start practising early, and practise every day.

## Advice from passers-by

People love to stop and chat when there's a pram involved. And they have questions.

*"Is he not cold?"*

*"Should he be out in this?"*

*"Should* you *be out in this?"*

I'm honestly not sure what the alternative is. Keep him indoors until he's one? Hibernate until spring?

In moments like these, I've adopted the wisest Irish philosophy I know:

*Ah sure, it'll be grand. We're not made of sugar.*
{: .pullquote}

## The lovely bit

But for every "is he not cold?", there are ten people who stop just to coo at him. Older ladies, dog walkers, neighbours. They peek into the pram and tell me what a gorgeous baby he is.

I always leave those little chats with a smile.

## Why I'll keep going out

I've always been an outdoorsy person, and getting out every day has been the best thing I've done for myself since he was born.

It stops the days from bleeding into one long blur on the couch. The crisp air, the brisk walk, the dog bounding ahead. It does more for my mental health than any app ever could.

## And the baby?

Through the wind, the rain, the sideways pram and the startled dog, what does he think of it all?

He's fast asleep. Every single time. The moment the pram starts moving, he's out like a light.

I may have accidentally discovered the greatest life hack in parenting. I'm not telling my dad, though. He probably already knows.
